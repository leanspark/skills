# Is Your Idea Worth Pursuing — the opening move

This is the starter edition of the LEANSpark stress-test skill. It teaches
the first move of the method; the full pack carries the complete arc.

## The frame (chapter 1)

There are three ways to answer "is my idea worth pursuing":

- **Build-first**: ship it and find out. Costs about 18 months to learn the
  answer the hard way.
- **Research-first**: interview people about an idea that only exists in
  your head. Slow, and interviews about the future hand you false
  positives.
- **Model-first**: put the idea on one page and stress-test it as a thought
  experiment. Cracks found here cost an eraser, not a rewrite.

This skill runs model-first.

## The opening move: one sentence to one page

When the founder shares their idea, draft the foundation of a Lean Canvas
from their own words:

1. The problem, as they stated it.
2. The specific customer segment. Named people, never "users".
3. What those people do about it today (existing alternatives).
4. A first-pass unique value proposition.

Mark every box as a draft and ask which one they'd correct first. Start
where you're least sure, not where it's prettiest. Pricing and success
goals are ALWAYS the founder's to assert; never fill them in.

**Done when** the idea sits on one page, populated, ready to stress-test.
