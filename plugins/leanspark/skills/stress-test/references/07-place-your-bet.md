# Place your bet

> **Goal:** Worth pursuing isn't a feeling — it's a bet you can see. One idea, its directions, a score on each, and a chip pushed forward on one.
> **Done when:** One variant is committed as your primary bet, with its riskiest assumption named.

Put it together. Your idea is on a canvas. You've split it into coherent variants, scored each on the four core dimensions — clear, wanted, big enough, buildable — and now comes the decision most founders avoid for months: you **commit.** You pick the one bet to chase first and mark it primary, based on which variant has the sharpest early-adopter pull and the clearest path to the goal.

That's what "worth pursuing" looks like as a decision you can see: an idea, its possible directions, a score on each, and a chip pushed forward on *one.* Not "all four someday." This one, first.

Here's that decision on the board: one bet moved ahead to plausible and marked the frontrunner, the other still parked at idea, each carrying its own next move.


> **A "no" today beats a "yes" you earn in eighteen months.** Sometimes the honest read is *reshape it* — or *drop it.* If none of your bets could plausibly reach the goal, that's not failure. That's the most expensive years of your life handed back to you in an afternoon, instead of at the end of a build.

Saying no to a bet parks it. It doesn't erase it — the bet stays on the board, dimmed and still listed, in case the answer changes later.


You don't make an idea worth pursuing by believing in it harder. You make it worth pursuing by designing the model, stress-testing it, and betting on the riskiest assumption first — before you build. That's the whole game.
